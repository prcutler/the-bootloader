### PartForge AI (Paul #1)

PartForge.AI is a new service that uses AI prompts to design 3D parts. You can use natural language to 
describe the part you want to design, or upload a photo, sketch or 3D file.

It has a free plan or plans start at $20 / month or you can bring your own API key from your AI service of your choosing. I designed one part with one prompt and probably 3 or 4 more edits, and it used about 40% of my session usage and 30% of my weekly usage.

Once the part is designed, PartForge makes it easy to edit, either by using natural language or by the sliders available to edit the project. There are sliders for the width, depth, wall thickness, and more. You can view and spin it from all angles just like using a normal CAD program. This is one of the reason they call projects forges - just like a real forge, you 

![Part Forge screenshot showing the part and sliders](partforge-1.png)

One of the things that was impressive is the recommendations it makes, such as adding a lip to the holder I was designing, or using a chamfer to round the edges. It even reminded me to add a cutout for the USB-C power cable that I forgot.

It outputs the design in STEP, STL, or 3MF and prompts you to use a license, including all rights reservered or more open licenses like MIT or Creative Commons. It also creates a GitHub repository you (or other depending on your license) can check out that has Javascript files with the details of the forge.

### M-VAVE FM-1 synth and custom firmwares (Tod #1)

Oh no another tiny synth toy, the M-VAVE FM-1. And you may've even heard about
this one, as it's been blowing into other hacker/maker forums because of what
people are doing with it. The FM-1 is a handheld, battery-powered synth 
with a 27-key silicone piano-like keyboard and 8 knobs and a nice color TFT display
and BLE. It came out about three months ago. It's designed as an "FM" synth, 
meaning it sounds like the venerable Yamaha DX-7 and can even load DX-7 patches. 
All this for only $70. And then hackers got to it.

People wondered if the FM-1 was running "Dexed", an efficient open-source emulation 
of the original DX-7. (There's even PicoDexed, a Dexed for the Pico) 
A week or so after it came out, hackers started poking around the FM-1's firmware update file and found it was indeed using Dexed. And then the reverse engineering really
begun. We learned the chip in the FM-1 was a JieLi AC791N, a 320 MHz dual-core RISC CPU 
that was evolved from a copy of a Blackfin DSP from the early 2000s but has grown
in functionlity towards working like modern ARM cores. 
The chip devkit likely used to develop the FM-1 was found on AliExpress
so devs could hack on a fully open board.
The various bootloader modes were discovered, as well as the [complete pinout
for all the buttons, knobs, LCD, audio codec, etc](https://github.com/fabiomorena/fm1-custom-fw-hub/blob/main/REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md). 

As this was happening, someone named "Baud girl" released [FM-1+VA](https://baudgirl.com/work/FM-1+VA), a complete alternate firmware for the FM-1 that turned it into
a virtual analog synth with a beautiful UI, a great sequencer, and sounded great. 
Suddenly your little $70 toy could be something totally new!  And you could go 
back to the stock firmware at any time. 

A week or so after that, the first open source custom firmwares for the FM-1 
started showing up, like the [Felucca firmware](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca)),
which turned the FM-1 into a 4-track groovebox with 13 different synth engines. 
Or [Sloop](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1), another groovebox that looks & works
much like the Teenage Engineering OP synths. The fascinating thing about
all these is they not only included a web installer for the firmware, but also
included a web-based emulator of the FM-1 with that firmware so you could try the
firmware out online. 

The [/r/MVaveFM1 subreddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/MVaveFM1/)
started having daily posts of new firmware. In response, 
websites popped up to manage the flood: 
an [atlas of firmware](https://wero1414.github.io/atlas-fm-1/) 
or an [online meta firmware catalog & installer](https://fm1-editor.com/firmware/).
It became a joke on the various synth sub-reddits that we don't need any new
synths because there will just be another FM-1 firware that has those features.

Almost all of the the firmware work is vibe coded, and proudly so. It's enabled
those who know barely enough but have an idea to realize that idea and scratch
their particular itch. So now we have [Ghoulbox dungeon synth](https://github.com/jasonpersinger/ghoulbox-fm1-dungeon-synth) firmware, a fork of Felucca that is
"tuned for dark ambient, medieval and fantasy synth music, 
from Old Tower and Hole Dweller to Mortiis-era dungeon synth". 

It appears much of the reverse engineering was done with the help of AI too. 
The particular JieLab chips have very little open documenatation and what does exist
is in Chinese. I think the original reversing efforts were done in China, so they
probably found it easier, but being able to dissabled a firmware image file
and find out the audio codec routines is the kind of thing that used to take months.
Now we can do it in a weekend. 

So the FM-1 is pretty fun, and custom firmwares have made it even more so.
Watchnig the reverse engineering efforts in near real-time has been a blast.
I think what's going on with the FM-1 is a bellwether of things to come. 
Thanks to these new AI systems, all hardware is pretty open now, 
even if the manufacturer doesn't want it to be. For customers this increases our
ability to customize and repair the products we buy and that's a good thing.
Companies will have to figure out how to deal with this going forward.
M-Vave, the company that makes FM-1, is responding well, releasing their own
[open source custom firmware called FM1 Open](https://forum.m-vave.com/topic/4)
and having an official rep join the /r/MVaveFM1 subreddit. They're seeing 
what's going on and how interest in the FM-1 has exploded because of this new-found
openness.

### Sprout Pi for Classic iPods (Paul #2)

Do you own an original iPod or iPod mini? The Sprout Pi Mainboard is a drop in replacement mainboard for iPod 3rd or 4th generation or iPod mini - no soldering required.

Sprout Pi replaces the logic board, runs new software, and includes new features, such as:

- Wi-Fi
- Bluetooth
- Hi-Fi DAC
- USB-C charging
- MicroSD card that can hold up to 2TB of music
- AirPlay and Google Cast

There are two versions, Sprout Pi and Sprout Pico. The former uses a Raspberry Pi Compute Module Zero and the Sprout Pico uses an rp2350.

Sprout is also open source - both the hardware and software. Schematics, board layouts, and the bill of material are licensed under CERN-OHL-S v2. Firmware and software and the Sprout Pi runtime are licensed under MPL-2.0. Documentation, including reverse-engineering notes on the iPod display, click wheel, and headphone connectors, are licensed under CC-BY-4.0.

### DVinyl (Paul #3)

DVinyl is a new web app that uses Docker to help you catalog your music, movies, books, games, or LEGO 
collections. If you can get it installed with an SSL certificate, you can even scan a UPC from your phone 
to add an item to your collection. It also can import your collections from Discogs, Hardcover, and more.

When you add an item, you can choose whether it's a paperback or hardcover if it's a book, for music you can choose from cassettes, vinyl records, and CDs, and for movies you can choose from DVD, Blu-Ray, VHS, or 4K Blu-Ray.

It has a well designed UI and makes it easy to add items, see your collection stats at a glance, and more.

- [DVinyl GitHub repository](https://github.com/Kyonew/DVinyl)
- [DVinyl demo site](https://demo.kyonew.me/)
