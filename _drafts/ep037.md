### HyperHDR (Paul #1)

HyperHDR is an open source ambient lighting system to install behind your television. By splitting your HDMI signal with one feed
going to the TV and the other to a USB video capture device connected to a Raspberry Pi or PC, it matches the LED colors to what is being displayed 
on your TV for an immersive experience.

### Meshtastic and Meshcore and the 900 MHz band (Paul #2)

[Tom Nardi at Hackaday had a great article](https://hackaday.com/2026/09/17/fcc-ism-rules-may-shatter-lora-mesh-communities/) a couple weeks breaking down the impact of FCC regulations 
on the 900 MHz Industrial, scientific and medical (or ISM) band and what it might mean for the Meshtastic and Meshcore projects.

As Tom shares, it turns out that the default radio configurations may be in violation of the Federal Communications Commission's regulations 
that have been around since the 80s. The radios need to operate above the 500 kHz band, and currently work at 62.5 kHz for MeshCore 
and 250 kHz for Meshtastic.

Due to bugs and issues with Meshtastic, [Philly Mesh announced they were switching to MeshCore](https://phillymesh.net/2026/09/02/fcc-regulations/). This will impact users running older radios who will no longer be able to communicate with the newer hardware.  It will be interesting to watch PhillyMesh over time to see how the transition works.

### Broke Beats: cheap music hardware (Tod #3)

As you know, I love inexpensive music gadgets. I make them!  Buy them please!
But there's two groups of people going much harder on the cheap music gizmo 
thing than I ever could: Cardputer hackers and Chinese guitar pedal makers. 

The first group are the various hackers making audio apps 
for the M5Stack Cardputer. You may remember me talking about the Cardputer
back in 2024 on Episode 9 of The Bootloader. The Cardputer is a little ESP32-S3
WiFi device with a built-in screen, keyboard, speaker, sdcard slot, and battery. 
Since then, M5 has released the Cardputer ADV, similar to the original, still $29,
but has a headphone/line out port!  This makes it entirely more useful. 
And in the intervening years, a whole app ecology has built up around the
Cardputer and similar boards. Point your browser at the Launcher Project, 
install its "GRUB-like bootloader", then choose from literally hundreds of different apps
for the Cardputer. Search, click, upload, and the Cardputer becomes a whole new thing. 
And this is all in the browsesr, via WebSerial.

But the most amazing thing in the Launcher firmware catalog is the BKLVA Pocket DAW app.  It's a full 4-track digital audio workstation on a teeny tiny screen. 
It's got a sampler, a drum machine, a six-voice synth, a sequencer with arranger
and song mode. And with mixer with audio effects like delay & reverb. 
All free, all running on this $30 handheld gadget. And because it's a little
embedded gadget not running an OS, it comes up immediately and you can just 
turn it off. Fits in your pocket so you can sketch music ideas anywhere. 
Or erase it all and install on the Cardputer the MiniAcid app, 
a little TR-808 & TR-303 emulator sorta like Propellerheadas Rebirth app. 
Or maybe instead install a webradio MP3 player. It only takes a few seconds to flash new firmware. Cardputer ADV plus BKLVA is highly recommended.

The other group of people going at the cheap music gizmo space are the 
China-based, usually sold only on Aliexpress, companies that are producing
little synths and guitar pedals.  One I really like is the Livtra Nanocore, 
it's an guitar effects boxl. It's got a big color screen and two knobs, 
battery-powered and with USB-C & Bluetooth. Like a pedal, it's got
mono inputs and outputs. It can do 8 simultaneous effects and are chained together
internally. The effects are everything a guitarist usually needs: 
amp simulators, compressor, drive & distortion, delay, chorus/flanger/tremolo, reverb, 
eq.  And each effect's paramters are adjustable and an entire group of effects
can be saved as a preset. These things are going for around $50 on Aliexpress.
It's incredible.  I don't have one yet, but the UI looks pretty reasonable.
I'm hoping to get one for myself this Christmas.

Both the BKLVA DAW app and the Nanocre gadget I learned about from the
"Broke Beats" series that's on SonicState.com, one of the better music gear
websites out there. Links to the gadgets and their articles in the notes.
