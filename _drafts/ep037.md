### HyperHDR (Paul #1)

HyperHDR is an open source ambient lighting system to install behind your television. By splitting your HDMI signal with one feed
going to the TV and the other to a USB video capture device connected to a Raspberry Pi or PC, it matches the LED colors to what is being displayed 
on your TV for an immersive experience.

### Meshtastic and Meshcore and the 900 MHz band (Paul #2)

[Tom Nardi at Hackaday had a great article](https://hackaday.com/2026/09/17/fcc-ism-rules-may-shatter-lora-mesh-communities/) a couple weeks breaking down the impact of FCC regulations 
on the 900 MHz Industrial, scientific and medical (or ISM) band and what it might mean for the Meshtastic and Meshcore projects.

As Tom shares, it turns out that the default radio configurations may be in violation of the Federal Communications Commission's regulations 
that have been around since the 80s. The radios need to operate above the 500 kHz band, and currently work at 62.5 kHz for MeshCore 
and 250 kHz for Meshtastic.

Due to bugs and issues with Meshtastic, [Philly Mesh announced they were switching to MeshCore](https://phillymesh.net/2026/09/02/fcc-regulations/). This will impact users running older radios who will no longer be able to communicate with the newer hardware.  It will be interesting to watch PhillyMesh over time to see how the transition works.

Links:

* [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2026/09/17/fcc-ism-rules-may-shatter-lora-mesh-communities/)
* [Meshtastic](https://meshtastic.org)
* [MeshCore](https://meshcore.io)
* [Philly Mesh switches to MeshCore](https://phillymesh.net/2026/09/02/fcc-regulations/)
