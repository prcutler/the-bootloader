### Terry Godier (Paul #1)

In *A Room You Can’t See*, Terry asks: what has changed in how we listen to music - the technology or us? Without spoiling too much, Terry digs into what does it mean when we have an endless supply of music at our fingertips from streaming services for one monthly fee? Where is what, he calls, the intentionality, of what we listen to.  He asks himself when the last time he really listened to an album. Sat down and paid attention to it - not in the background when working or in the kitchen.

He even touches on the music being streamed on Spotify that is AI - and Terry says he’s not mad about the music, he’s mad that it even works and these songs get millions of listens.

Terry challenges you to think about your relationship with music in a video that is about 10 minutes long and really makes you think.

[A Room You Can’t See video essay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GzzNBPqZhw0)

His second video is about design and it’s called “Some Decisions Aren’t Supposed to Be Optimized” - and he asks when did we hand over design decisions to the machine? He talks about how, for him, design is a feel. He tells a story about how a designer at Google quit back in 2009 because Google was testing, not one, not three, but 41 different shades of blue through A/B testing. He talks about how testing could be answering - and asking - the wrong questions.  It really makes you think into how and why decisions are made in your favorite products.

[Some Decisions Aren’t Supposed to be Optimized video essay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-FrnyEggE4k
)

After watching his videos, I checked out his blog and realized I was using one of the products he created. Earlier this year, and I’ve been debating about sharing it on the show even, Terry released Current, an RSS reader for macOS, iOS and iPadOS.  Current has a really interesting goal - that your RSS feed shouldn’t feel like an inbox an dshow you the unread count. You should read your RSS feed for the joy of reading and stay in the flow while doing it. I switched to it earlier this year after buying it for $10, and I’ve been really happy with it. What a small world it is sometimes.

### AP30 Music Boy (Paul #2)

Next up I have the AP30 Music Boy, which may jsut be the most adorable retro gadget I’ve seen in some time. You’ll notice that it’s called the “Music Boy” and that’s definitely on purpose as this MP3 player looks like a Gameboy, from the screen to the buttons. It’s pretty small as it sits in the palm of your hand and features 2.0” IPS screen. It’s Hi-Res Audio certified and they really push how high quality the music is supposed to sound. It feature’s a 3.5mm and 4.4mm headphone jacks as well as Bluetooth 5.3 and you can install a microSD card to store your music.

You can stick it in your pocket, hang it on a lanyard, or it comes with a magnetic case to stick to the back of your phone. You can also use it as USB DAC on your computer to punch up the sound quality.

### Headphones and more headphones (Paul #3)

Paul's last two are headphones related. The first has been in the news for a bit, but was still new to me. Are you in the Android or Linux ecosystems and ever been jealous of the functionality Apple's AirPods have? Well, thanks to a project called [LibrePods](https://github.com/librepods-org/librepods), you can now use AirPod like features on Linux and Android.

The developers used Wireshark to reverse engineer Apple's proprietary protocols. You can use features like aut-connect, listening mode, battery status, and more.

* [LibrePods GitHub page](https://github.com/librepods-org/librepods)

And my last one is via Tom’s Hardware, which recently covered the Sony Head Tracker project by Nicholas Slattery on GitHub. If you have a high end pair of Sony’s noise canceling headphones, like the Sony WH-1000XM5’s, you’re in luck. 

These headphones have support for spatial audio, meaning they have sensors including gryoscopes and acceleromoters for head tracking. Mr. Slattery has reverse engineered the sensors firmware to allow you to use headtracking in games on macOS and Windows.  Once you have everything setup, you put your Sony headphones on and the in game camera will respond to your head movements - which is so cool.

Check out the article to see the list of headphones that are compatible and for more details on how it works.

* [Tom’s Hardware coverate of Sony Head Tracker](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/pc-gaming/you-can-now-use-your-sony-headphones-as-a-real-time-head-tracker-for-race-and-flight-simulators-on-pc-several-hundred-games-already-supported-enthusiast-creates-open-source-app-that-translates-live-sensor-data-into-in-game-camera-controls)
* [Sony Head Tracker on GitHub](https://github.com/NicholasSlattery/sony-head-tracker)
