Welcome to The Bootloader, I'm Paul Cutler.

And I'm Tod Kurt. If you haven't heard of us, we're the hosts of The Bootloader podcast which you can find in your favorite podcast player on the first Monday of each month or at thebootloader.net. The show works like this: Paul and I have each brought a few things to share, all about CircuitPython from the last year. But first, Paul, break down some of the numbers.

Paul
It's crazy that another year has gone by already. Since last CircuitPython Day, CircuitPython 10 was released with a ton of improvements. This was the first release to add some audio effects, full Fruit Jam support, and a bunch graphics updates, including improving `picodvi` to work on more monitors.

Tod
(A couple sentences about the audio effects)

We then saw 10.1 in February and 10,2 this past April. 10.2 included the MicroPython 1.27 merge, so thanks to Dan for keeping up with upstream.

Tod
(Thank you to MicroPython and support them on GitHub)

Paul, what's your first one?

Paul
If there was one theme over the last year, is that it was the year of the Fruit Jam. This credit card sized CircuitPython powered microPC was a hit. It was first released in July of 2025, but there weren't a lot the first few months, and then in the last Adabox late last year Adafruit shipped them out to all the subscribers.

I'm not going to go into all the hardware specs, you probably know them by now, but Tod, let's share a few of our favorite Fruit Jam apps from the last year. My favorite is probably Sean Carolan's Pac-Man clone. Originally written for the Wio terminal, Cooper Dalrymple ported it to the Fruit Jam and upstreamed it to Sean. Sean vibe coded Pac-Man in an afternoon and it's fantastic port. I had him on a recent episode of The CircuitPython Show and he shared where he got the game resources and how he re-created the sounds of Pac-Man. I'll have to admint to losing a few hours playing it.

Tod
(Dan Cogliano and CP Z-Machine)

Paul
Dan's been busy. In addition to the Z-Machine, he also released a handful of classic screensavers, and another game, [Moon Miner](https://danthegeek.com/2025/12/28/moon-miner-learn-guide/). It's related to the classic Lunar Lander game from the 80s. Your mission is to retrieve minerals from the moons around the solar system. It's all physics based - you drive your rocket ship based on how much thrust you're giving it and the gravity below. You need to make a safe landing to harvest the minerals - and you can't tip over either!
